## Memory is a market

A phone number, price or web address only works if people can recall it at the moment they need it. Random strings are hard to remember; patterns are easy. Short-term memory comfortably holds only a handful of items, so we compress numbers into chunks — and repeated digits, mirrored pairs and sequences compress beautifully. *333 3333* is effectively one chunk; *481 9267* is seven.

## Vanity and repeating phone numbers

Businesses have long paid premiums for phone lines that are easy to remember or that spell a word. Memorable numbers can make radio, outdoor and word-of-mouth advertising more effective, because a listener who cannot write the number down still has to recall it later. Many telecom regulators run auctions or premium allocations for “golden” numbers for exactly this reason.

## Lucky and unlucky digits

Culture changes the value of digits. In Chinese-speaking markets, **8** is associated with prosperity because of its sound, and **4** is often avoided because it sounds like the word for death. The opening ceremony of the 2008 Beijing Olympics famously began at 8:08 pm on 8/8/08. Many buildings across East Asia skip the fourth floor, just as many Western buildings skip a 13th floor. **7** is lucky in much of Western culture, and **9** is associated with longevity in Chinese because of a similar sound-alike effect.

## Numeric domain names

Short numeric .com domains have a well-established secondary market, driven in large part by buyers in China, where numbers are easier to remember and type than English words. Short, repeating and “lucky” combinations sell at the highest premiums. A six-digit .com with a strong repeating tail — such as 193333 — sits inside that tradition: compact, pattern-rich and language-neutral. If you are evaluating a numeric name, the main value drivers are length, repetition, lucky-digit composition, and whether the number has an obvious use (a phone-number match, a date or a postal code).

## How to choose a memorable business number

- **Repeat or mirror digits** where you can (e.g. 3333, 1212).
- **Avoid locally unlucky digits** in markets you serve.
- **Keep it short.** Every extra random digit costs recall.
- **Test it out loud.** If someone can repeat it after hearing it once, it works.
